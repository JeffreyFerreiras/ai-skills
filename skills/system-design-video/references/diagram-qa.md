# Diagram QA for animated technical lessons

Use this when a lesson animates a diagram, a moving request, a code card, or a dashboard. It supplements the review loop and the selected style. It does not replace them, and it does not add a review round. The chalk TikTok gates that sit on top of this page are in [the series reference](chalk-tiktok-series.md).

Preserve approved artwork, transitions, and audio while you fix a defect. Test the explanation the viewer sees, not only the last local edit.

## Before animation

List every object, its stable identity, who owns it, and every connector with a source port, a destination port, a direction, and a path id. Trace every route at full resolution and at phone size, including the outer cable, the collar, and the opaque tip. Pointing at a database is not touching it.

Treat generated art as unverified geometry until that trace is done. Freeze the approved assets, the audio, the renderer, and a checksum manifest before a repair, and work in a new version.

Semantic boxes may stay simple. A detailed resource must not collapse into a placeholder between scenes. A physical screen needs a plausible perspective, content that is mapped onto it, and no mirrored text or anatomy that the scene did not intend.

## One model for drawing and motion

Draw the cable, place the plug, and move the request on the same path. Reverse that path for the return when the system returns on it. Test the source-to-cable join, the cable-to-plug join, and the opaque tip against the target. A numeric tolerance does not excuse a visible gap.

Include the full padded bounds of cards, label backs, caption strokes, glows, trails, and rings through every move, fade, and transition. Docking and occlusion are allowed only for the object pair and the time range you named.

Queues keep unique slots and stable identities through arrival, departure, and reflow. A rejection has to travel against the occupied slots, not through them. Counts on screen come from the same state as the objects.

Bind a repeated narration cue to the scene and the occurrence. Do not attach every "save" to the first match in the file. Enforce the causal order for that topic. For a pool, acquire happens before the query, the result or the error happens before release, and a waiter timeout cannot release some other holder.

Run the frame checks through dense bursts and transitions. A failed check exits nonzero. Keep a known-bad fixture that reproduces a historical failure, and keep the command output. Separate a defect that appears on many frames from the one defect it is. A printed diagnostic is not a passed gate.

## Evidence from the encode

Bind the report to the exact MP4 path, its hash, the runtime, the dimensions, and the frame rate. Inspect frames decoded from that file. Source art and renderer stills do not replace them.

Cover endpoint closeups for every connector, phone-size frames, the worst clearance, and consecutive frames around each motion and each transition. Say which stretches you did not cover.

Check composited pixels through fades, not only the declared alpha. Consecutive frames of a transition keep one copy of the scene text. If a crossfade of two text-bearing scenes double-exposes the type, use a fade through the background or a cut. Verify the canvas alpha you actually got.

For the chalk series, also reject burned-in scene ids and track labels, reject limbs, and keep captions in the caption band. Those checks are specified in the series reference. Timestamps in review notes are how you point at a moment.

## Code cards and dashboards

Highlight with a lexer token stream. Reconstruct the source, including newlines and indentation, and change classification only on whole tokens. Check the reconstruction and the encoded phone-size contrast.

Say which snippets are illustrative excerpts and which program compiled. The notes name the imports, the injected dependencies, and the types the card omits. Check lifecycle claims against the documentation you cite. Registering a service is not a promise that it was released. Object pooling and connection pooling are different mechanisms.

Give the code a reading beat. Do not shrink it because the narration got shorter. Keep code, captions, and emphasis off each other.

A mock dashboard keeps an example-data label and a neutral design. Do not invent a vendor's product UI or imply an account is connected. Distinguish a metric the product emits from one you would have to configure. Counters on the mock come from the same simulation as the motion. Admission rejection and pool timeout are different outcomes.

## Pacing

Audition a narrated sample early, and audition the full encoded timeline before delivery. Intelligibility and word endings are separate from the loudness number.

Measure the silence at paragraph boundaries on the encoded mix. A configured pause plus the synthesizer's own padding can become a much longer gap. Inspect the waveform before you trim. Do not assume the export stretched the voice unless the waveform shows it.

When a pace change is authorized, trim combined silence or adjust articulation in a way that keeps pitch and identity. Rebuild captions and cues from the new waveform. Do not speed the whole MP4 to fix one gap.

On this skill's general lessons, missing sentence pauses are a more likely cause than the wrong synthesis speed, and a short sample still prefers one continuous take. The chalk TikTok series overrides that with one take per sentence and the gap band in the series reference. Do not apply the series band to other lessons.

## Repair and release

Resolve an approximate timestamp from the encoded frames around it. Do not retune an unrelated scene because the timestamp was loose.

Say which identity you checked: source pixels, decoded waveform, audio packets, or the bitstream. Report only the check you ran.

A still or a strip is not continuous playback. When a video-review pass of the actual timeline is available, use it for motion and listening. Stills cannot support a claim that the video was watched. If two reads disagree, consecutive encoded frames decide the interval they cover, and only that interval.

The release note names the artifact hash, the checkpoint, the methods used, the tests, and any accepted cosmetic exception. An open blocker stays open. Sample approval does not mean every test is clean.

Do not burn scene identifiers or track labels into any copy. Keep the caption and the cover as separate files from the master. Publication is its own approval. For this series, Jeffrey uploads to TikTok himself.
