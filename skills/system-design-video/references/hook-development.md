# Hook development

Develop and review the hook early in every new lesson, before script lock and
final narration. Keep it in the production document alongside the source claims,
storyboard, first-frame design, promised payoff, and review findings. This step
does not authorize publishing, extra generation, or redesigning accepted work.

## Promise, picture, and payoff

Choose a recognizable problem, consequential question, or accurate promise the
lesson actually answers. Make the opening understandable without unexplained
terms. Give an immediate visible example or consequence, then connect it to the
contextual what/problem/why/solution introduction and roadmap in teaching order.
Avoid manufactured urgency, false guarantees, or a question never answered.

Design the words and first phone-safe picture together. A cover may animate into
the first seconds if its image and concise text support the hook. It is optional;
do not add a long static title card, unrelated logo, or decorative delay. Check
the exact first encoded frame for crop, readable text, contrast, and subtitle
placement at delivery size, then inspect opening motion and the handoff to the
next scene through temporal evidence. A still cannot establish motion quality.

Story review checks the reason to continue and the actual payoff. Technical and
fresh-context logical review check wording, visual implications, and causality.
Visual review checks composition and opening motion; voice review checks emphasis
and the introduction join when narration is present. Share the existing maximum
of three rounds per asset, stop at the first clear round, and report unresolved
material issues at the cap. A prediction that a hook is compelling is not measured
retention or proof that viewers stop scrolling.

## Existing narration and copy

When narration is already approved, prefer a coherent visual/text hook over the
existing voice if it carries the promise accurately. Do not automatically replace
or regenerate narration to add an opening. Preserve its hash, cues, identity,
natural delivery, and unaffected timing. If a spoken change is actually needed
and authorized, make the smallest coherent repair, review both joins, and retime
captions/anchors from the resulting waveform.

For a shadow-traffic lesson, a visual question such as
`TESTS PASSED. READY FOR REAL TRAFFIC?` can introduce the existing narration.
The payoff must explain what real-traffic observation adds and its limits, rather
than claiming test passage or response isolation proves safety. A candidate that
does not answer customers can still write or trigger side effects; retain the
lesson's isolation and side-effect safeguards.

Do not use em dashes in audience-facing copy, including hook text, scripts,
captions, cover text, titles, descriptions, and handoff messages. Use a sentence
rewrite, comma, or period without changing the technical meaning. Review the
actual authored and encoded copy, not just the planning document.
